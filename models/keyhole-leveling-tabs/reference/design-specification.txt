# Design Specification: 3D-Printed Keyhole Leveling Tabs

## 1. Objective

Design a family of miniature, 3D-printable leveling tabs for a wall-mounted digital picture frame.

The frame hangs from two screws using keyhole-shaped mounting slots on its back. One side hangs slightly lower than the other.

The tabs should allow the user to raise one side of the frame in precise **1/32-inch increments**, without relocating the wall screws or modifying the frame.

**Deliverable:** A set of eight individual STL files, a parametric source model, and a combined print plate compatible with Bambu Studio and the Bambu Lab P2S.

## 2. Mechanical Concept

Each tab is a single, rigid, T-shaped component consisting of:

1. **Adhesive mounting pad:** A thin rectangular plate that attaches to the back of the frame using double-sided tape.
2. **Short inward-facing leg:** A small protrusion extending from the mounting pad into the existing keyhole opening.
3. **Load-bearing contact surface:** The leg creates a new contact point for the screw, changing the height at which the frame hangs.
4. **Identification bumps:** Small raised dots on the exposed face of the mounting pad identify the tab's offset.

### Important mechanical requirements

- The leg must be **very short and compact**.
- The leg projects inward into the keyhole, rather than extending outward toward the wall.
- The tab must not obstruct insertion of the 0.30-inch screw head through the keyhole's wider opening.
- The load-bearing leg must engage the keyhole geometry and transfer the frame's weight reliably.
- The adhesive pad primarily retains and positions the tab; the design should not depend solely on tape peel strength.
- The tab must not interfere with the frame sitting close to the wall.
- The leg and pad should form a single printed component, with a filleted transition to reduce stress concentration.

### Coordinate system

Use the back of the frame as the XY plane:

- X: Horizontal across the frame.
- Y: Vertical, positive upward.
- Z: Perpendicular to the frame, positive toward the wall.

The adhesive pad lies in the XY plane. The leg projects into the keyhole recess in the negative Z direction.

The exact contact geometry must be determined from the actual keyhole dimensions. Do not assume that a simple vertical extension automatically produces the desired hanging offset.

| Printer | Bambu Lab P2S |
| Nozzle | Standard 0.4 mm |

### Dimensions requiring confirmation

- Narrow keyhole slot width.
- Keyhole recess depth.
- Wide opening diameter.
- Distance between the top of the narrow slot and the wide opening.
- Screw shank diameter.
- Screw-head projection from the wall.
- Available flat adhesive area around the keyhole.
- Frame weight and material surrounding the keyhole.

These dimensions should remain configurable until verified.

## 4. Parametric Tab Geometry

### Adhesive pad

Initial dimensions (adjustable):

- Width: 0.60 inches (15.24 mm).
- Height: 0.30 inches (7.62 mm).
- Thickness: 0.06 inches (1.52 mm).
- Corner radius: 1 mm.
- Flat rear surface suitable for double-sided adhesive tape.

The pad should be as small and unobtrusive as practical.

### Inward-facing leg

The leg should:

- Be centered horizontally relative to the adhesive pad.
- Fit inside the narrow keyhole slot with appropriate clearance.
- Project only as far into the recess as needed to engage the load-bearing geometry.
- Use rounded edges and a reinforced root.
- Provide the desired vertical offset without blocking the screw head.

**Critical:** Vary the leg's effective contact position, not merely its overall physical size.

A longer or thicker leg is acceptable only if it changes the hanging position in the intended direction.

### Offset variants

Generate eight distinct, fixed-geometry tabs:

| Variant | Offset (inches) | Offset (mm) | Bumps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1/32 | 0.794 | 1 |
| 2 | 1/16 | 1.588 | 2 |
| 3 | 3/32 | 2.381 | 3 |
| 4 | 1/8 | 3.175 | 4 |
| 5 | 5/32 | 3.969 | 5 |
| 6 | 3/16 | 4.763 | 6 |
| 7 | 7/32 | 5.556 | 7 |
| 8 | 1/4 | 6.350 | 8 |

Each tab must be a single fixed-height piece.

**Do not design an adjustable, sliding, ratcheting, or interchangeable mechanism.**

## 5. Identification Bumps

Each tab should have a unique number of tactile bumps corresponding to its position in the series.

Requirements:

- 1 bump for the smallest offset, 8 bumps for the largest.
- Raised hemispherical or rounded cylindrical dots.
- Approximately 0.8–1.0 mm diameter.
- Approximately 0.4 mm protrusion.
- Arrange in a compact, consistent grid.
- Bumps must not interfere with adhesive placement or keyhole engagement.
- Keep bumps on the visible, non-adhesive face.

The bumps should be identifiable by sight and touch.

## 6. Materials and Printing

### Printer

Bambu Lab P2S with a 0.4 mm nozzle.

### Preferred material

PETG, for toughness and resistance to brittle fracture.

### Suggested print settings

- Layer height: 0.12 mm.
- Walls: 4.
- Infill: 100% for these small components.
- Supports: Avoid where possible.
- Brim: Optional.
- Orient to maximize strength at the leg-to-pad junction.

The CAD agent should evaluate print orientation so that the leg does not fail through weak interlayer adhesion.

All eight variants should fit on a single print plate.

### Dimensional accuracy

Model the offsets at their exact metric equivalents.

Use Bambu Studio's normal layer quantization and dimensional compensation as appropriate. Verify actual contact heights after printing.

## 7. Structural and Fit Requirements

The agent must evaluate:

1. Whether the leg engages the keyhole securely.
2. Whether the screw head can still enter and seat properly.
3. Whether the tab is mechanically retained by the keyhole or depends on adhesive to support the frame.
4. Whether the adhesive is loaded in shear or peel.
5. Whether the screw head and shank contact the intended surfaces.
6. Whether the tab can slip, rotate, deform, or disengage during use.

If the proposed geometry relies on tape to support the full frame weight, explicitly flag this and revise the design toward positive mechanical retention.

Do not assume the keyhole supports the tab merely because the leg sits inside the opening.

## 8. CAD Implementation

Use a parametric modeling system such as:

- OpenSCAD, preferred for reproducible scripted geometry.
- CadQuery, acceptable alternative.

Expose parameters for:

- `pad_width`
- `pad_height`
- `pad_thickness`
- `leg_width`
- `leg_depth`
- `leg_contact_offset`
- `keyhole_slot_width`
- `keyhole_depth`
- `screw_head_diameter`
- `screw_shank_diameter`
- `clearance`
- `bump_count`
- `bump_diameter`
- `bump_height`

All dimensions should be modeled internally in millimeters.

Use one shared model function to generate all eight variants.

## 10. Acceptance Criteria

The design is complete when:

- Eight distinct offsets are generated in exact 1/32-inch increments.
- Every tab has the correct number of identification bumps.
- The leg is short and projects inward into the keyhole.
- The adhesive pad remains low-profile.
- The screw-head clearance is preserved.
- The tab creates the intended vertical displacement.
- The CAD model is fully parametric.
- All STL meshes are manifold.
- The files import successfully into Bambu Studio.
- The design includes a clear installed cross-section showing the actual load path.
- A physical test confirms that the tab supports the frame securely without relying on an unverified adhesive joint.

## 11. Development Sequence

**Phase 1 — Geometry validation**

Create a dimensioned cross-section of the original keyhole, wall screw, and proposed tab. Establish how the new contact point changes the frame's vertical position.

Do not finalize the load-bearing leg before validating this geometry.

**Phase 2 — Prototype**

Generate three prototypes: 1/32", 3/16", and 1/4".

Verify fit, installation clearance, and actual offset.

**Phase 3 — Production set**

Generate all eight variants, identification bumps, STL files, and the combined 3MF print plate.

**Phase 4 — Documentation**

Provide renders, installation instructions, measured tolerances, and the complete parametric source.

### Design priority

Favor **minimal size, simple geometry, positive mechanical engagement, accurate height offsets, and reliable 3D printing** over visual complexity.

The finished product should resemble a tiny adhesive-backed T-shaped insert, not a conventional picture hanger or a large adjustable bracket.
